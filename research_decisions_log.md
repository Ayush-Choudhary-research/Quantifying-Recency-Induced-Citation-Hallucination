# Research Decisions Log
**Topic:** Knowledge Cutoff Effects on LLM-Generated Literature Reviews in Rapidly Evolving Fields

## Phase I: Prompt 1 - Workspace and Review Scope
*   **Status:** Initialized
*   **Review Scope Summary:** 
    This project investigates the systemic vulnerabilities introduced by fixed training horizons (knowledge cutoffs) in Large Language Models (LLMs) when used to generate literature reviews in fast-moving scientific domains. The literature review verified three primary failure modes: 
    1. Coverage staleness (systematic under-representation of recent work).
    2. Cutoff-boundary and forgetting failures (temporal leakage during ex-ante reasoning).
    3. Citation hallucination triggered specifically by recency pressure. 
    Current mitigations like Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) and knowledge editing are only partially effective. A major identified limitation in current research is the lack of standardized temporal-freshness metrics and longitudinal benchmarks for review generation.

## PROMPT 5: Research Questions and Success Criteria

**Research Problem**
While it is known that LLMs fabricate bibliographic citations, it remains unclear how directly a model's parametric knowledge cutoff exacerbates this failure mode. This study investigates whether the recency pressure of emerging research topics forces a statistically higher rate of citation hallucination compared to historical topics well within the model's training distribution.

**Testable Research Questions & Hypotheses**
*   **RQ1:** How does the citation hallucination rate of a baseline LLM (`gemini-3.1-flash-lite`) differ when generating literature reviews for historical (pre-2022) topics versus emerging (post-2025) topics?
    *   *Hypothesis:* Hallucination rates will be significantly higher for post-2025 topics because the model lacks parametric exposure to the relevant literature but is aligned to comply with the prompt's structural demands.
*   **RQ2:** Do the failure modes (e.g., completely fabricated titles vs. real authors paired with fake titles) differ between historical and recent topic hallucinations?

**Variables and Evaluation Metrics**
*   **Input Data:** `topics_dataset.json` (5 pre-2022 topics, 5 post-2025 topics).
*   **Independent Variable:** Topic temporal category (Historical vs. Emerging).
*   **Dependent Variable:** Citation Hallucination Rate (%).
*   **Evaluation Metric:** `Hallucination Rate = (Unverified Citations / Total Generated Citations) * 100`. Verified via Semantic Scholar API matching.
*   **Meaningful Success Threshold / Decision Rule:** A measured difference of >15% in hallucination rates between the two temporal groups will be considered evidence of recency-induced degradation.
*   **Contradicting Observation:** If the pre-2022 topics yield equal or higher hallucination rates than the post-2025 topics, it would contradict the hypothesis that knowledge cutoffs are the primary driver of this specific hallucination spike.

**Distinguishing the Contribution**
*   *Already Demonstrated:* LLMs hallucinate citations (Alkaissi & McFarlane, 2023), and APIs can detect them (Abbonato, 2026).
*   *My Investigated Contribution:* A controlled comparative measurement isolating the *temporal variable* to quantify how much of that hallucination rate is driven specifically by post-cutoff recency pressure.

## PROMPT 6: Dataset and Baseline Validation Sheet

**1. Primary Dataset & Measurement Source**
*   **Input Dataset:** `topics_dataset.json` (Curated list of 5 pre-2022 and 5 post-2025 computer science topics).
    *   *Source/Access:* Local file, manually curated.
    *   *File Structure:* JSON dictionary with string arrays.
*   **Ground Truth Verification Oracle:** Semantic Scholar Academic Graph (S2AG) API.
    *   *Source to Verify:* official Semantic Scholar `/graph/v1/paper/search` endpoint.
    *   *Licensing/Access:* Free tier academic access (Approved). Requires `x-api-key` header.
    *   *Compute Requirement:* < 10 MB RAM for JSON parsing; heavily network-bound.

**2. Baseline Methods & Comparators**
Instead of comparing two different machine learning models, this study uses a single model under two controlled conditions.
*   **Control Baseline:** Gemini API (`gemini-3.1-flash-lite`) generation on **Pre-2022 Topics**. This establishes the natural hallucination rate for information securely within the model's pretraining distribution.
*   **Experimental Target:** Gemini API (`gemini-3.1-flash-lite`) generation on **Post-2025 Topics**. This tests the degradation caused by recency pressure.

**3. Evaluation Design**
Since we are not training a model, standard train/validation/test partitions do not apply. We will use an **Inference-Only Benchmark Protocol**:
1.  **Generation Phase:** Loop the standardized prompt through the LLM for all 10 topics.
2.  **Extraction Phase:** Use a regular expression (Regex) or structured output parsing to isolate the generated Titles and Authors.
3.  **Verification Phase:** Query the S2AG API. A citation is marked "VERIFIED" if the generated title fuzzy-matches an existing title in the database AND at least one author matches. It is marked "HALLUCINATED" if the API returns zero matches or a complete mismatch.

**4. Leakage Risks**
*   **Time-Level Overlap (Pretraining Contamination):** `gemini-3.1-flash-lite` may have a training cutoff extending into 2026. If so, "post-2025" topics might not be entirely out-of-distribution. We must acknowledge this limitation; we are testing recency, not necessarily absolute zero-shot ignorance.
*   **Data Leakage:** Testing the same topic twice could yield cached or biased results. We will set the API temperature to `0.0` (greedy decoding) to ensure deterministic, reproducible outputs.

## PROMPT 12 & 14: Contribution, Evaluation, and Interpretation

**Chosen Contribution (Pathway B Executed)**
A controlled comparative study measuring citation hallucination rates across two distinct temporal windows (historical vs. emerging). The baseline is preserved across both conditions (identical LLM `gemini-3.1-flash-lite`, `temperature=0.0`, identical structural prompt), isolating the topic's temporal emergence as the sole independent variable.

**Frozen Experimental Results (`experiment_results.csv`, 50 target citations)**
*   **Historical (`pre_2022`):** 25 target citations | 20 evaluated (5 `API_ERROR` excluded) | 18 `VERIFIED`, 2 `HALLUCINATED_AUTHOR`, 0 `HALLUCINATED_TITLE` $\rightarrow$ **10.00% Hallucination Rate** ($2/20$).
*   **Emerging (`post_2025`):** 25 target citations | 25 evaluated (0 `API_ERROR`) | 19 `VERIFIED`, 5 `HALLUCINATED_AUTHOR`, 1 `HALLUCINATED_TITLE` $\rightarrow$ **24.00% Hallucination Rate** ($6/25$).
*   **Measured Effect Size:** **+14.00 percentage-point increase** ($2.40\times$ relative surge).

**Interpretation of Outcomes**
*   **Directional Support & Threshold Nuance:** Citation hallucination more than doubled ($2.40\times$) when moving from historical (`10.0%`) to emerging (`24.0%`) topics. Because 5 historical queries encountered unresolved Semantic Scholar `API_ERROR` timeouts (reducing the `pre_2022` denominator to $N=20$) and 2 historical entries triggered top-3 author-matching mismatches, the observed $+14.00$ percentage-point difference sits $1.00$ percentage point below our strict pre-registered $>15.0\%$ decision threshold—demonstrating strong directional degradation under recency pressure alongside baseline API verification noise.
*   **Mechanism of Failure (RQ2):** Across all 8 flagged hallucinations, $87.5\%$ ($7/8$) are `HALLUCINATED_AUTHOR` and $12.5\%$ ($1/8$) are `HALLUCINATED_TITLE`. Pure title fabrication occurred exclusively in `post_2025`, while parametric author-title conflation dominated both windows.
 
## PROMPT 16: Critical Technical Peer Review

### Reviewer 1 — Experimental Design & Verification Oracle
* **Strengths:** Clean single-variable isolation (holding `gemini-3.1-flash-lite`, `temperature=0.0`, and prompt structure constant across temporal windows), explicit handling of HTTP 429 rate-limit dropouts (`API_ERROR` excluded from the denominator rather than misclassified), and empirical taxonomy separating `HALLUCINATED_AUTHOR` ($87.5\%$) from `HALLUCINATED_TITLE` ($12.5\%$).
* **Major Critique 1 (Title-Similarity Blind Spot in S2AG Matching):** The verification script queries `/graph/v1/paper/search` with the generated title and checks whether the generated author appears in the top 3 returned records, without verifying string similarity between the queried title and the returned paper's title. If Semantic Scholar returns an unrelated paper by the same author in the top 3 hits, a fabricated title could be falsely labeled `VERIFIED`.
* **Major Critique 2 (Asymmetric `API_ERROR` Attrition):** Five `pre_2022` citations were dropped due to HTTP 429 throttling ($N=20$ evaluated) compared to zero dropouts in `post_2025` ($N=25$ evaluated). If those 5 historical papers were all genuine foundational works, the true `pre_2022` hallucination rate would be $2/25 = 8.0\%$ rather than $10.0\%$ (yielding a $+16.0$ percentage-point gap, above the $15.0\%$ threshold).

### Reviewer 2 — Statistical Power & Scope
* **Strengths:** Honest reporting that the observed $+14.0$ percentage-point difference ($10.0\%$ vs. $24.0\%$) falls $1.0$ percentage point short of the pre-registered $>15.0\%$ decision boundary, avoiding post-hoc threshold shifting.
* **Minor Critique 1 (Sample Size & Confidence Intervals):** With $N=20$ and $N=25$ evaluated citations, 95% Wilson confidence intervals overlap substantially ($[2.8\%, 30.1\%]$ for `pre_2022` vs. $[11.5\%, 43.4\%]$ for `post_2025`; two-sided Fisher's exact test $p = 0.2692$). The manuscript should frame the $2.40\times$ jump as a strong pilot effect requiring larger-scale replication ($N \ge 200$ citations per window).
* **Minor Critique 2 (Temporal Overlap in `post_2025` Topics):** Several topics labeled `post_2025` (e.g., Direct Preference Optimization, test-time compute scaling) have seminal 2023–2024 preprints, meaning the benchmark captures fast-moving recency pressure rather than strict post-cutoff zero-shot behavior.
